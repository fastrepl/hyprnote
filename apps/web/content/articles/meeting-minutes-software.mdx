Meeting minutes software can produce an AI recap, help a secretary record decisions, or manage the approval of formal board minutes. Those are different jobs. A transcript preserves what was said; useful minutes make decisions, owners, deadlines, and unresolved questions easy to find.

Start with the record your team needs after the meeting. If everyone needs a searchable recap, an AI notetaker may fit. If a committee must approve an official record, look for editing, approval, access controls, and an archive before choosing a transcription engine.

This comparison covers nine options using their published product and pricing documentation, reviewed in October 2026. It is not a hands-on accuracy benchmark. Prices and plan limits can change; the linked vendor pages are the place to confirm a purchase.

## How to choose meeting minutes software

Decide which of these workflows you need:

- **Personal AI notes:** capture a conversation, review the summary, and keep your own record.
- **Team meeting management:** prepare shared agendas, record decisions, and follow up on assigned tasks.
- **Formal minutes:** review a draft, approve it through the required process, and control access to the final record.
- **Customer-call follow-up:** connect a recap and action items to the correct CRM records.
- **A searchable meeting library:** retrieve transcripts, recordings, or clips with appropriate sharing and retention settings.

These can overlap, but an AI summary does not become approved minutes automatically. Agree on who reviews the draft and who can approve it. Our [meeting notes templates](/blog/meeting-notes-template/) include a minutes format you can use to compare outputs across tools.

Recording method and processing location are separate choices. A desktop app can capture a meeting without a bot and still send audio to a cloud service. Check where transcription, summaries, storage, and sharing happen individually.

## Detailed reviews of meeting minutes software

### 1. Anarlog - local meeting data and a choice of AI

![Anarlog](anarlog/blog/library/products/anarlog/desktop/2026-08-20-meeting-summary.jpg)

[Anarlog](https://anarlog.so/) captures desktop audio without a meeting bot and combines your notes with a transcript to draft a summary. Canonical sessions, notes, and transcripts live in local SQLite; recordings and attachments are separate local files. Markdown is an export format.

The useful distinction is control over both storage and AI providers. You can use local models, bring your own provider keys, or choose hosted AI. Local storage alone does not mean processing stays on the device: check transcription and summary settings separately. Optional encrypted CloudSync, hosted processing, and sharing each have their own data paths.

Choose Anarlog when you want a personal meeting record you can export and an AI configuration you can control. It does not replace a board portal's formal approval process. For an offline setup, follow the [local transcription and summary configuration guide](/blog/offline-meeting-transcription-mac/) and test it before a meeting.

**Pricing:** [Free covers local-model and bring-your-own-key workflows; Pro is $14/month or $140/year](https://anarlog.so/pricing/). Provider usage can have its own costs.

### 2. Fellow - shared agendas and team follow-up

![Fellow Ask AI panel beside shared talking points and assigned action items](anarlog/blog/library/products/fellow/ask-ai/2026-08-27-talking-points.webp)

[Fellow](https://fellow.ai/) combines shared agendas, meeting notes, action items, and a searchable meeting library. Ask Fellow can help retrieve meeting context and prepare follow-up material. This suits teams whose problem starts before the call and continues into the next recurring meeting.

Fellow is not restricted to a visible meeting bot or online calls: its published options include botless desktop and mobile capture, including in-person conversations. Confirm which capture mode works on your team's devices and which recording, access, and retention policies administrators can enforce.

**Pricing:** [A free plan is available; Team is $7/user/month billed annually or $11 monthly](https://fellow.ai/pricing). AI notes and recording allowances differ by tier. Check the allowance for your intended workflow before assuming every recurring meeting is covered.

### 3. MeetGeek - a meeting library and team analytics

[MeetGeek](https://meetgeek.ai/) offers transcripts, summaries, action items, templates, and a meeting library with team analytics. It is worth evaluating when several people need to search past conversations and turn meeting output into follow-up workflows.

Fireflies organizes transcripts, summaries, search, and integrations in a shared workspace. Evaluate it when colleagues need to find discussion context and send follow-up into other systems.

## Test the minutes, not just the transcript

A tool can transcribe most words correctly and still miss a decision, reverse a negation, or assign a task to the wrong person. There is no cross-product accuracy percentage established by this comparison.

Use a representative meeting with participants' permission and run the same review for each shortlisted tool:

1. Check names, technical terms, numbers, dates, and speaker attribution against the source.
2. Separate proposals from agreed decisions. Mark unresolved questions instead of allowing a summary to imply agreement.
3. Check each action's owner and deadline. Use our [meeting action items guide](/blog/meeting-action-items/) to build a consistent follow-up format.
4. Correct the draft and take it through the required review or approval process.
5. Export the final record, test recipient permissions, and check how long the transcript and recording remain accessible.

If formal approval is essential, test that workflow first. If the main job is personal recall, prioritize a readable draft, reliable retrieval, and portable exports.

## Check processing, permissions, and retention separately

Map the path from capture to transcription, summary generation, storage, and sharing. Ask which services receive audio or text, who can decrypt stored material, and which administrators or link recipients can read it.

Transport encryption protects a connection; it does not by itself prevent the service at the other end from reading the content. End-to-end encryption has a different boundary: only the intended endpoints can decrypt the protected material. The [EFF's encryption guide](https://ssd.eff.org/module/what-should-i-know-about-encryption) explains this distinction. Check which data a product's encryption claim actually covers.

Compliance language also needs scope. A SOC 2 report, GDPR obligations, and HIPAA requirements are different things; a badge does not establish that a particular meeting workflow meets your organization's requirements. [HHS does not recognize private HIPAA Security Rule certifications](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/are-we-required-to-certify-our-organizations-compliance-with-the-standards/index.html). Review the applicable contracts, controls, retention settings, and access policies.

## Choose the workflow you can maintain

Shortlist Diligent, Beenote's governance tier, or MeetingBooster when a formal review and approval process is central. Compare Fellow's shared agendas when preparation and recurring follow-up are the problem. Evaluate MeetGeek, Fireflies, Fathom, or tl;dv for searchable call output and integrations.

If you want local meeting data and control over transcription and summary providers, [try Anarlog](https://anarlog.so/download/). Start with one meeting, review the decisions and actions, and export a copy. The useful result is a record people can trust and use after the call.
