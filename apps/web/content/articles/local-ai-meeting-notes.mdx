Local AI meeting notes need more than a recorder that runs on your laptop. Speech recognition, summary generation, storage, and sharing can each use a different processing path.

This guide compares Anarlog, Meetily, Shadow, Talat, and Screenpipe by those boundaries. Anarlog and Meetily let you select local speech and local summary models. Talat includes local models by default. Shadow keeps core transcription on the Mac but uses external providers for AI features. Screenpipe combines local capture with configurable AI and optional cloud services.

Product documentation and prices were checked on October 12, 2026. These are documented capabilities, not hardware or accuracy benchmark results. For desktop versus server deployment, see the [self-hosted notetaker guide](/blog/selfhosted-ai-notetakers/); for other workflows, browse our [transcription guides](/guides/transcription/).

## How to set up an offline Anarlog meeting

Local transcription and local summaries are separate choices. Selecting Ollama for summaries does not change where your audio is transcribed.

![Anarlog Settings → Transcription with the model dropdown open, showing on-device options](anarlog/blog/library/products/anarlog/desktop/2026-09-23-settings-transcription-dropdown.png)

1. **Check model availability.** The built-in on-device transcription paths require a supported Apple Silicon Mac. Intel Macs, Windows, and Linux need a separate Deepgram-compatible local server or a hosted speech provider. Open **Settings → Transcription** and check the available models for your installed release. Apple Speech additionally requires macOS 26 and a supported language.
2. **Prepare speech-to-text.** Download a Soniqo model and select it under **Model being used**. Alternatively, configure Apple Speech's language assets or choose **On-device file** with a downloaded whisper.cpp `.bin`. Read the model's **Live** or **After recording** label; after-recording models do not produce a live transcript.
3. **Prepare summaries separately.** Download a text model in LM Studio, Ollama, or Unsloth, start its local server on the same computer, and select it in **Settings → Intelligence**. Choose a model that fits your available memory alongside the meeting app. Experimental Apple Intelligence is another option on eligible Macs running macOS 26 or later.
4. **Run a short offline check.** After all downloads finish, disconnect from the internet and record your own short sample. Verify capture, transcription, summary generation, and export. If a step fails, inspect its selected provider and the local server before relying on it in a meeting.
5. **Review optional data paths.** For a local-only workflow, leave CloudSync, sharing, Cloud API & Connectors, and hosted providers disabled. Avoid Chat's web search: it sends queries to a hosted endpoint even when both selected models are local.

The canonical meeting record is stored in local SQLite; recordings and attachments are separate local files. Markdown is an export format. Enabling a hosted AI provider sends the content needed for that operation to that provider. Optional encrypted CloudSync and the server-readable Cloud API & Connectors copy are distinct services; review each before enabling it.

Sources: Anarlog's [offline setup](https://docs.anarlog.so/offline), [model compatibility](https://docs.anarlog.so/models-and-providers), and [privacy policy](/privacy/). This is a setup procedure you can verify on your own hardware, not a claim that every model or operating system supports offline transcription.

## Five local meeting workflows

### 1. Anarlog: meeting notes with a selectable AI stack

![Current Anarlog meeting summary view](anarlog/blog/library/products/anarlog/desktop/2026-08-20-meeting-summary.jpg "char-editor-width=80")

[Anarlog](/) captures microphone and system audio without adding a meeting bot. You can write a memo during the call, then generate a summary from your notes and the transcript. Sessions and text live in local SQLite, while recordings and attachments are separate files. Markdown is a portable export.

**Local path:** select a supported on-device speech model and a local Intelligence model independently. A whisper.cpp file is for processing after recording; it does not provide a live Whisper transcript. Intel Macs, Windows, and Linux can connect to a Deepgram-compatible local server instead of relying on built-in Mac speech models. The setup above covers the supported Mac path.

**External path:** your own API keys and Anarlog Cloud use hosted providers. Encrypted CloudSync, sharing, the Cloud API, calendars, and chat web search also have their own data paths. Review [security](/security/) and the current [model documentation](https://docs.anarlog.so/models-and-providers) before choosing a configuration.

**Tradeoff:** running speech and summary models together uses your computer's resources. Test your actual model choices and meeting length. Choose Anarlog when you want manual notes, transcripts, summaries, and exports in one local workspace.

**Pricing:** [Free](/pricing/) includes local and BYOK workflows; provider charges are separate. Pro is $14/month or $140/year and includes managed AI, encrypted CloudSync, sharing, and calendar integrations.

### 2. Meetily: local capture with separate Community and Pro editions

![Meetily workspace with transcript, summary, decisions, and action items](anarlog/blog/articles/local-ai-meeting-notes/image-20.png "char-editor-width=80")

[Meetily Community](https://github.com/Zackriya-Solutions/meetily) captures microphone and system audio, transcribes with Whisper or Parakeet, and imports recordings. Windows and macOS have installers; Linux Community is built from source.

**Local path:** speech runs on your hardware; select Ollama for local summaries. macOS packages use Metal/CoreML. The packaged Windows Whisper build uses Vulkan; CUDA requires a configured NVIDIA source build rather than automatic selection by the installer.

**External path:** BYOK summaries send transcript text to the selected provider. Enable exports and integrations only for destinations you want to receive that content.

**Tradeoff:** the current [edition comparison](https://meetily.ai/pro/) puts speaker identification, custom templates, advanced PDF/DOCX/Markdown exports, and MCP/webhook/CLI integrations in commercial Pro. Speaker identification is available in Pro, rather than a universally unfinished feature.

**Pricing:** Community is free under MIT. Pro is $10 per user per month billed annually, or $25 regular monthly; Enterprise is custom. Check the edition before assuming the MIT download includes every Pro capability.

### 3. Shadow: Mac meeting capture with external AI Skills

![Shadow action-item extraction panel with task assignments](anarlog/blog/articles/local-ai-meeting-notes/screenshot-2026-04-10-at-7-43-38-pm.png "char-editor-width=80")

[Shadow](https://www.shadow.do/) records on the Mac without a participant bot. It detects calls, captures meeting screenshots, and supports editable prompts called Skills. It is a fit when visual context matters alongside the transcript, but it is not the only screen-capture tool in this comparison.

**Local path:** its [privacy guide](https://www.shadow.do/help/privacy-and-data) documents on-device audio capture, transcription, speaker diarization, and a local Markdown/media vault.

**External path:** automatic meeting titles, Action and Meeting Skills, Ask, Share to Web, and webhooks can send the relevant transcript, screenshot, notes, or other context. A locally captured screenshot becomes external data when selected as context for an external feature.

**Tradeoff:** for a local-content workflow, disable automatic titles and automated Skills, avoid Ask and external sharing/webhooks, and review inputs before running a Skill. Keeping transcription local does not establish offline AI summaries.

**Pricing:** [Free](https://www.shadow.do/pricing) includes unlimited transcription, audio recording, and Smart Screenshots, with a two-week Plus trial. Plus adds unlimited AI features; Shadow's [current billing comparison](https://www.shadow.do/blog/shadow-vs-bluedot-bot-free-meeting-assistant-2026) lists $8/month annually or $12 month-to-month.

### 4. Talat: local defaults on Mac and Windows

![Talat meeting summary with timestamped topics and key discussion points](anarlog/blog/articles/local-ai-meeting-notes/image-25.png "char-editor-width=80")

[Talat](https://talat.app/) supports macOS 15+ on Apple Silicon and Windows 10+. It captures both sides of calls, supports recording imports, and offers Apple and Google calendar integration. Built-in summaries now use Qwen3.5-4B, with optional alternative providers.

**Local path:** recording, speech recognition, speaker handling, summaries, and the meeting database run locally by default. Its [capabilities page](https://talat.app/capabilities) also documents screenshots taken with a hotkey during a recording, with local text recognition and placement in the transcript. This differs from Shadow's automatic meeting-window screenshots and Screenpipe's broader desktop history.

**External path:** the [privacy policy](https://talat.app/privacy) separates optional cloud LLM requests and Google Calendar access from local meeting content. It also documents a diagnostic/license ping carrying installation, version, platform, onboarding, and license/trial details. No third-party behavioral tracking does not mean no network traffic.

**Tradeoff:** check optional providers, calendars, webhooks, and export destinations before treating the entire workflow as local. Test speaker labels and summaries on a representative call.

**Pricing:** ten hours of recording are free to try. Current options include $89/year and a $189 lifetime license, plus monthly billing. Once the recording allowance ends, existing meetings remain searchable, exportable, and available for summaries; new recording requires a paid plan.

### 5. Screenpipe: local desktop history with broader capture scope

![Screenpipe meeting transcript beneath a shared project document](anarlog/blog/articles/local-ai-meeting-notes/image-26.png "char-editor-width=80")

[Screenpipe](https://github.com/screenpipe/screenpipe) combines screen context and audio into searchable computer history on macOS, Windows, and Linux. Meeting notes are one use of that history rather than the whole product.

**Local path:** local SQLite/media storage, Whisper speech, and local AI such as Ollama. Accessibility data and screenshots add screen context; MCP and scheduled workflows can use that history.

**External path:** cloud transcription, AI, sync, integrations, and organization-managed storage can transmit data. The repository also documents product analytics and crash diagnostics, with an Analytics setting to disable telemetry. A local capture database does not make an attached cloud assistant local.

**Tradeoff:** the capture scope is broader than a meeting recorder. Review filters, permissions, retention, and AI access, then measure resource use on your machine instead of assuming a fixed monthly storage budget.

#### Source license and app subscriptions

The current [source license](https://github.com/screenpipe/screenpipe/blob/main/LICENSE.md) is the Screenpipe Commercial License. It allows personal non-commercial use, nonprofit/education/research use, and a seven-day organizational evaluation. Commercial source use requires a paid license. Official prebuilt apps have separate subscription/app terms. Previously published MIT versions retain MIT; that does not make the current source MIT-licensed.

Current [app pricing](https://screenpipe.com/onboarding) offers Free, Basic at $250/year, and Business at $500 per seat/year, with monthly and capacity options. Business includes personal device sync and team seat management; shared context across teammates is not currently included. The README says existing lifetime licenses remain valid, while new lifetime purchases are no longer sold.

## Choose the workflow, then verify the boundary

- **Notes plus a transcript:** Anarlog pairs your memo with captured meeting audio and lets you select speech and summary providers.
- **Local capture and summaries:** Meetily offers a free Community route; check Pro for speaker identification and advanced exports.
- **Visual meeting context:** Shadow automates meeting screenshots, while Talat documents manual screenshot capture. Screenpipe covers broader desktop activity.
- **Local defaults with packaged models:** Talat includes a local summary model and supports Mac and Windows.
- **Searchable computer history:** Screenpipe connects screen and audio history to AI tools; review capture scope and current license terms first.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Granola a local AI notetaker?

Granola captures audio on a device but uses cloud transcription and AI providers and stores transcripts and notes in its hosted infrastructure. Its [security page](https://www.granola.ai/security) describes those boundaries. Device capture alone does not establish local processing. Compare [Granola alternatives](/blog/granola-ai-alternatives/) if you need different storage or AI choices.

### Can I use local meeting notes offline?

Prepare speech and summary models before disconnecting, then verify both stages. Local capture and transcription do not guarantee local summaries or integrations. Shadow's external AI features need a connection; the configurable tools require the correct provider selections. Check activation and model-download requirements separately.

### Are local models as accurate as cloud services?

There is no universal winner established by this comparison. The model, language, audio quality, overlapping speech, and available resources matter. Test a sample that resembles your meetings and inspect the transcript, speaker labels, and summary before switching.

### Does local storage mean nothing leaves the computer?

No. Hosted AI can receive meeting content, a webhook can transmit an export, and diagnostics can send device metadata even when the main record lives locally. Check each enabled path rather than relying on the word local.

### What hardware should I check?

Check the app's platform requirements and the speech and summary models together. Anarlog's built-in speech models require supported Apple Silicon hardware; its local-server alternative is separate. Leave resources for your conferencing app and test a recording of the length you need.
