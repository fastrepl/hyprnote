The best transcription software for Mac depends on what you need to record. An audio file, a live meeting, and a podcast editing project need different capture and export tools.

For **free file transcription**, start with MacWhisper. For **live meetings with a local meeting archive**, choose Anarlog and configure a supported local model. For **occasional voice recordings**, try Apple's built-in Notes or Voice Memos before installing anything. Descript fits text-based media editing; Otter and Notta fit cloud meeting workflows.

## Does macOS transcribe audio for free?

Yes, on supported hardware and in supported languages. Apple's tools cover more than dictation:

- **Notes** can record audio, show a transcript, search it, and copy it into a note. Its [macOS Tahoe 26 guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/notes/record-and-transcribe-audio-apdb5106e334/4.13/mac/26) lists M1 or later and device languages including English, Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, French, German, Japanese, Korean, Simplified Chinese, and Traditional Chinese. Apple Intelligence can summarize a recording when enabled and eligible.
- **Voice Memos** lets you view and [copy a recording's transcript](https://support.apple.com/guide/voice-memos/vm4a03609f0d/mac) into another document. It is useful when the recording already lives in Voice Memos.
- **Live Captions** displays spoken and computer audio as text on Apple Silicon. [Availability depends on language and region](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/mchldd11f4fd/mac). It serves a captioning workflow rather than a structured meeting archive.

For an occasional recording, try these first. For a folder of existing audio files, speaker-labeled transcripts, or a meeting workflow that captures microphone and system audio together, compare the dedicated apps below.

## 1. Anarlog: best for live meetings with local control

[Anarlog](/) is an open-source AI meeting notetaker. It captures microphone and system audio without adding a bot to Zoom, Teams, or Google Meet. Canonical meeting data lives in local SQLite; Markdown is an export format.

![Anarlog Settings → Transcription on macOS with the speech-to-text providers you can configure](https://static.anarlog.so/blog/library/products/anarlog/desktop/2026-09-23-settings-transcription.png)

*Choose the speech model in Settings → Transcription.*

### Free and offline setup

On supported Apple Silicon Macs, built-in transcription has no monthly minute quota. Select Parakeet Streaming for live text or Parakeet Batch for after-recording transcription. On-device file transcription accepts a downloaded whisper.cpp `.bin` model. Apple Speech requires a compatible macOS 26 setup. See [platform requirements](https://docs.anarlog.so/desktop-installation) and [model setup](https://docs.anarlog.so/help#use-on-device-models).

On Intel Macs, configure a supported provider or local Deepgram-compatible server. BYOK is included, but provider usage may cost extra. For summaries to work offline too, select a local Intelligence model such as Ollama or LM Studio and prepare it before disconnecting.

[Pro costs $14/month or $140/year](/pricing), adding hosted AI, encrypted CloudSync, sharing, and calendar-connected services. Local storage, search, templates, and provider choice are available without that subscription.

**Choose it for:** bot-free meetings and local ownership, with explicit control over the AI data path.

## 2. MacWhisper: best for private audio and video files

[MacWhisper](https://macwhisper.com/) is a Mac app for drag-and-drop file transcription, microphone recording, and dictation. It uses local speech models by default. Using an open-source engine does not make the MacWhisper application an open-source project.

![MacWhisper transcript with local AI tools for summarizing and analyzing the text](https://static.anarlog.so/blog/library/products/macwhisper/transcription/2026-08-30-ai-tools.png)

*MacWhisper's transcript editor and optional AI tools. Source: [MacWhisper](https://macwhisper.com/).*

### Free versus Pro

Free includes smaller local Whisper models, unlimited file transcription, and TXT, SRT, and VTT export. Pro adds larger models, batch processing, automatic speaker recognition, meeting recording, real-time captions, and advanced document exports. The official site lists a **€64 one-time Pro license**; App Store licensing and pricing differ.

Meeting recording is a Pro workflow, so MacWhisper is not limited to files. Its [privacy documentation](https://docs.macwhisper.com/article/52-keeping-transcriptions-private) also explains the local-only boundary: cloud transcription sends audio to the selected provider; cloud AI prompts or translation send transcript text. Local AI through Ollama or LM Studio avoids that cloud prompt path.

Speed depends on your Mac, model, and recording. Check a representative file before processing a backlog; this guide does not provide measured hardware benchmarks.

**Choose it for:** file transcription with local processing and a one-time upgrade option.

## 3. Descript: best for podcast and video editing

[Descript](https://www.descript.com) uses a transcript as an editing surface: delete words and the corresponding audio or video is removed. It fits creators who need a finished media project as well as a transcript.

![Descript text-based editor with waveform, transcript, and AI Tools panel](https://static.anarlog.so/blog/library/products/descript/editor/2026-08-30-transcription.png)

*Descript's text-based editor, waveform, and AI tools. Source: [Descript](https://www.descript.com/transcription).*

Its [pricing comparison](https://www.descript.com/pricing) lists **60 media minutes per month on Free**. Media minutes count imported or recorded content, including content you do not transcribe. They are separate from the AI credits used for AI features.

Paid plans list Hobbyist at $16/month with annual billing or $24 monthly, with 10 media hours; Creator at $24 annually or $35 monthly, with 30 hours; and Business at $50 annually or $65 monthly, with 40 hours. Prices are per person. Export quality and AI allowances vary by plan.

**Choose it for:** text-based editing. For a transcript-only local workflow, a file transcriber is a closer fit.

## 4. Otter: best for cloud meeting capture and follow-up

[Otter](https://otter.ai) offers a cloud meeting workspace with calendar-connected bots and bot-free desktop capture options. Its Mac download does not make transcription local or offline.

![Otter Notetaker calendar controls, visible meeting participant, and live transcript workspace](https://static.anarlog.so/blog/library/products/otter/notetaker/2026-07-17-notetaker-overview.png)

*Otter's calendar, meeting participant, and transcript workflow. Source: [Otter](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/4425393298327-Otter-Notetaker-Overview).*

### Free-plan limits

[Basic](https://otter.ai/pricing) includes 300 monthly transcription minutes, access to the first 30 minutes of each conversation, three lifetime audio/video imports, and access to the 25 most recent conversations. Older conversations are archived rather than deleted.

[Supported languages](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/360047247414-Supported-languages) now include English, Spanish, French, German, Japanese, and Simplified Chinese. German and Chinese are beta; language selection is generally one language per conversation, with a French–English exception.

Pro is $16.99 per user monthly with 1,200 in-app recording minutes and up to 90 minutes per meeting. Business is $30 monthly with unlimited meetings and in-app recordings, up to four hours per meeting. Annual billing and regional promotions differ.

**Choose it for:** cloud collaboration and automatic capture. Check [our Otter review](/blog/otter-ai-review/) and [data-handling guide](/blog/is-otter-ai-safe/) before using it for sensitive meetings.

## 5. Notta: best for multilingual cloud transcription

[Notta](https://www.notta.ai) supports [58 monolingual transcription languages and 42 translation languages](https://support.notta.ai/hc/en-us/articles/4403155631131-What-languages-does-Notta-support). Bilingual transcription and real-time translation have their own supported languages and entitlements.

![Notta desktop and mobile interfaces showing AI notes, speakers, and transcript playback](https://static.anarlog.so/blog/library/products/notta/cross-device-transcription/2026-07-22-cross-device-transcription.webp)

*Notta's meeting record across desktop and mobile. Source: [Notta](https://www.notta.ai/en/features/ai-transcription).*

Its [pricing page](https://www.notta.ai/en/pricing) lists 120 free minutes per month, three minutes per conversation, and 50 file uploads. Regional free allowances can differ; check the account you will actually use. Transcript export is a Pro feature, so a nominal monthly allowance is not enough to evaluate a one-hour interview workflow.

The retrieved annual plans list Pro at $8.17/month with 1,800 minutes and up to five hours per recording, and Business at $16.67/month with unlimited transcription. Monthly prices and translation add-ons differ.

**Choose it for:** multilingual cloud workflows after testing language coverage, transcript access, and export availability.

## 6. Rev: best when you need human review

[Rev](https://www.rev.com) combines AI transcription with separately ordered human transcription. You can upload from a Mac browser without relying on a local speech model.

![Rev transcript document with speaker labels and timestamps](https://static.anarlog.so/blog/library/products/rev/transcription/2026-08-30-human-transcript.webp)

*Rev's transcript document with speaker labels and timestamps. Source: [Rev](https://www.rev.com/services/human-transcription).*

The current [pricing page](https://www.rev.com/pricing) lists 45 AI transcription and caption minutes per month on Free, with English-only transcription. Essentials is $29.99 per seat with monthly billing and 5,000 AI minutes per user; Pro is $59.99 monthly with broader language coverage and 10,000 AI minutes per user. Annual billing costs less.

Human transcription starts at **$1.99 per minute**. Rev advertises 99%+ accuracy and a standard turnaround of 12 hours or less; those are service claims, not a guarantee that a transcript is suitable for every legal or medical use. Verify critical wording yourself and select the appropriate service.

**Choose it for:** a human review service rather than an offline desktop tool.

## 7. Sonix: best for browser editing, subtitles, and translation

[Sonix](https://sonix.ai) provides a browser transcript editor with speaker labels, subtitle tools, and 54+ languages. Files are processed in the cloud.

![Sonix browser editor with synchronized video, speaker labels, timestamps, and transcript text](https://static.anarlog.so/blog/library/products/sonix/transcript-editor/2026-08-30-editor.jpg)

*Sonix's browser editor with synchronized playback and transcript text. Source: [Sonix](https://sonix.ai/transcription-software).*

[Pricing](https://sonix.ai/pricing) includes a one-time 30-minute trial, $10/hour pay-as-you-go, and Core at $25/month with five hours of transcription and translation. Annual billing is discounted. Additional subscription hours are $10/hour, and translation draws from the same allowance.

**Choose it for:** editing and repurposing transcripts in a browser, especially when you need subtitle or translation output.

## How to choose: files, live meetings, or editing

![Decision tree matching common Mac transcription needs to the right tool](https://static.anarlog.so/blog/articles/transcription-software-mac/mac-transcription-decision.png)

The decision tree groups common Mac workflows. Check the current table and product sections for platform and plan limits.

- **A recording already on your Mac:** start with MacWhisper. Test speaker recognition and the export format you need before buying Pro.
- **A live call with notes and a reusable meeting history:** use Anarlog with a supported local model, or Otter/Notta when a cloud workspace fits your team.
- **A short voice recording:** try Apple Notes or Voice Memos. Copy the text out before deciding you need another app.
- **A podcast or video to edit:** choose Descript for transcript-based editing; consider Sonix for subtitle and translation work.
- **A transcript requiring human review:** compare Rev's AI and human services separately.

## What to check before recording on a Mac

1. **Match your hardware.** Apple Silicon and Intel support are different. Check the app's minimum macOS version and the specific model's requirements.
2. **Prepare offline models.** Download transcription and summary models beforehand, then test with the network disconnected. Optional cloud AI, translation, sync, or integrations create separate data paths.
3. **Check capture permissions.** A file upload does not require the same permissions as microphone and system-audio recording. Make a short recording and confirm both sides of the conversation are audible.
4. **Test the output.** Verify names, technical terms, speaker labels, timestamps, and the required TXT, Markdown, document, or subtitle export. AI transcripts need review.
5. **Check the effective free limit.** Monthly minutes, accessible transcript length, file imports, and export access are separate limits.
